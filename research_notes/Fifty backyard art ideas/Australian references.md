# Australian backyard references

## Which building choices can you spot from a roof?

### Takeaway
Use real Victorian suburban houses and the way their lots fit together as visual references. Give three views around the fence genuinely different silhouettes.

### Cited Findings
- Museums Victoria's model of Wirringulla, designed in 1918 in Camberwell, shows two roof gables with green shingles, grey roof tiles, white stuccoed brick, a deep verandah and a sleeping porch. Its sliding windows and panelled glass door are documented too. [Museum model](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/247399)
- A Ringwood East family photograph circa 1965 documents a brick home, a red flower border and a personal house name commemorating a ship. [Museum photograph](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/1689782)
- The State Library's Melbourne and Metropolitan Board of Works plans record individual buildings, garden plots, property lines, rear lanes and drains. These are historic maps, an excellent basis for composing an invented neighbourhood. [State Library Victoria](https://www.slv.vic.gov.au/search-discover/explore-collections-format/maps/maps-melbourne-city-suburbs/mmbw-plans)

### Inferences
1. Upgrade one neighbour to a twin-gabled Camberwell-style bungalow, with moss-green gable ends and pale render. Start with the roofline visible from the arena ahead of fine trim.
2. Give the neighbour opposite it a side-on, long brick profile rimmed by one unbroken red flower bed. Players can call it the brick house.
3. Connect two background lots with a rear service lane. Two garage doors face one another across the lane, giving the tank-top view a clear vanishing point.
4. Add an enclosed sleeping porch to the shaded end of one house. A curtain and dusty sliding windows give it depth when seen from the ridge.
5. Design three original house-name plaques. Give just the nearest house one large enough to read from the rear roof, making it a small discovery.
6. Over the rear fence, show one long narrow block split off from the next by a dogleg boundary and diagonal garden path. Its silhouette suggests the district grew one lot at a time.

### Gaps
The references document individual properties, not the frequency of these features in all Australian suburbs. The proposed neighbourhood is fictional.

## What makes a garden look tended by somebody?

### Takeaway
A real western Melbourne family's garden links a vine pergola, home-woven baskets and sharing produce with neighbours. This gives artists one coherent scene at several distances.

### Cited Findings
- A 1980s St Albans photograph shows Salvatore Mazzarino cutting grapes from a vine pergola. He grew produce for family and neighbours and wove collection baskets from cane that he grew. [Museums Victoria](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/1950200)
- A 1958 Melbourne preserving-equipment booklet documents home preserving of fruit, sauces, jams and juices. [Museums Victoria](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/1484249)
- A colourless, small, embossed glass milk bottle made in North Melbourne was recovered at Casselden Place. [Museums Victoria](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/1665674)

### Inferences
7. Put a grape pergola behind the back fence, with a twisted trunk, hanging bunches and broad, broken shade on pale paving. Its overhead mass is readable from across the yard.
8. Hang a cane harvest basket from a fence hook, interrupted mid-repair with loose cane trailing from the rim.
9. Cut a small produce-pass hatch in a neighbour's dividing fence and show two bags of lemons marked with household initials, explaining how they share the harvest. Keep it scenery.
10. Dress a single kitchen window with amber and plum-coloured preserving jars. Depth comes from a dark interior and window highlights, using one painted interior card at distance.
11. Place cuttings rooting in mismatched embossed milk bottles on a laundry sill. Use drawn waterlines and stems plus broad highlights so the browser needn't sort overlapping transparent surfaces.
12. Give one household a pruning calendar by the flyscreen, with pencilled crop dates, a torn-off corner and a thumb-smudged patch at the current month. Readable graphic masses at distance; type only up close.

### Gaps
The hatch, labels, calendar, plants, placement and reuses are original designs. Historical photographs and trademarks should not be copied. No particular decade or suburb for Marcus's map has been established.

## Which plants or animals provide distinctive colour and movement?

### Takeaway
Choose eucalypt bark and wildlife behaviour precisely. Take the greenery down to several heights around a fence corner so that palms, generic Christmas-tree shapes and single-texture forests aren't required for variety.

### Cited Findings
- Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria describes Eucalyptus tricarpa as having deeply furrowed dark bark over its trunk and branches, and slender adult leaves. The identification key separately describes eucalypts which retain curls or ribbons of old bark. [VicFlora ironbark](https://vicflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/flora/taxon/46b30d64-1f21-4d71-a770-027910050d6c), [Victorian eucalypt key](https://vicflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/pages/eucalypts)
- BirdLife Australia's garden guidance describes habitat layers of grasses/groundcover, shrubs and trees. It highlights Acacia for insects, nectar plants for honeyeaters and native grasses for seed heads. [BirdLife Australia](https://birdlife.org.au/discover-birds/birds-in-your-backyard/bird-friendly-garden/)
- The Willie Wagtail hunts insects across lawns and flicks its tail sideways. Its white brow, black throat and white belly are identifiable. [BirdLife Australia](https://birdlife.org.au/bird-profiles/willie-wagtail/)
- Cicada nymphs climb a trunk or other surface near emergence, leaving empty skins behind. Adult cicadas can occupy trunks in summer. [Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/insects/cicadas-superfamily-cicadoidea/)

### Inferences
13. Improve the gum grove by differentiating species. Pair a dark, deeply grooved ironbark with a separate pale ribbon-shedding tree. Give the latter two conspicuous curling strips near a fork. This extends the existing generic bark work.
14. Build a planted verge in three heights, from native grass at its toe through a bottlebrush or grevillea shrub to the existing canopy. Keep the dense portion outside the fence, with its low warm seed heads visible at the boundary.
15. Set a cluster of small amber cicada skins at player eye height on the timber by the roof ladder. A hollow back seam tells the story in close-up without adding flickery wildlife particles.
16. A Willie Wagtail hunts along the mown strip. Give it two quick sprints and a pause with a sideways tail swing, so its body language differs from the existing birdbath magpies.
17. Build the canopy from narrow, hanging sprays of eucalyptus leaves with sky gaps through the crown and asymmetric branch groups. Use the silhouette for the far trees and the leaf shapes only nearby.
18. Place a nectar-feeding bird on the outermost grevillea flower. Bend that one stem under its weight and let it spring back on departure, for an event players can see and understand from the roof.

### Gaps
These are scoped art and ambient-animation proposals. No claim that the references prove any FPS, gameplay or usability improvement. Validate scale and sight lines from the running game.
