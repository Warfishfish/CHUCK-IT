# Fifty backyard art ideas and eight requested improvements

**For the reported problems, I’d start with #53 and #57, then rebuild the lawn in #52.** Fixing the flicker first gives you a stable view for judging grass and water. For the original art shortlist, I’d pick **#1, #22 and #41**. The three rooflines give players a way to find their bearings from the newly walkable ridge. Dazza’s sauce label is a small, original piece of art you can try in one session. The Heist wall patterns tackle a different problem: remembering the way home with a teddy after the bases move. For each, there’s a simple playtest. Can a mate point towards the pool from the roof, remember what was on the bottle, or call the right turn at a junction?

The normal desktop game uses the Polished look. The recent roof-review shots used a simpler comparison preset. The earlier Polished view already shows a row of coloured neighbouring houses, so the first proposal changes their outlines. The 50 entries below are proposals grounded in project source, game developers’ accounts and Australian collections. The project has separate Three.js and Rust/WASM browser entries. This review checked source and saved captures. ## Houses worth climbing the roof to see

Museums Victoria’s Camberwell model has twin gables, a deep verandah and a sleeping porch. Melbourne’s historic MMBW plans record rear lanes, garden plots and individual buildings. House House designer Nico Disseldorp described building the village in *Untitled Goose Game* from real places selected for the interactions each area needed. Those references suggest designing whole views from Marcus’s playable roof. [Camberwell house model](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/247399) · [State Library Victoria maps](https://www.slv.vic.gov.au/search-discover/explore-collections-format/maps/maps-melbourne-city-suburbs/mmbw-plans) · [House House interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-house-house-s-i-untitled-goose-game-i-)

1. **Three recognisable neighbour rooflines** — Put a green twin-gabled bungalow behind the pool, a squat hipped brick house behind the BBQ, and a long single-slope extension behind the shed. Players on the ridge can pick a direction by silhouette.
2. **A view down the back lane** — Line up two garage doors, a pair of tyre tracks and a distant streetlight beyond a gap between houses. From the tank lid, the neighbourhood will appear to continue past the map.
3. **A laundry window with depth** — Recess the main house’s rear window and hang a half-drawn blind in front of an amber interior and a folded blue towel. Walking sideways across the roof reveals one layer behind another.
4. **A glazed sleeping porch** — Enclose one end of a neighbour’s verandah with tall sliding panes. Leave one pane open and the curtain drawn to one side so the porch has a different silhouette at each end.
5. **Brick piers on the low side** — Expose two brick supports and a patterned air grille where the main house meets the lower part of the lawn. At blob height, the shadows between them give the building weight.
6. **A car restoration under the carport** — Give the long carport a primer-grey bonnet on trestles and a partly covered car body. The outline tells you what the owner spends weekends doing, even from across the fence.
7. **A tennis ball stranded in the gutter** — Set a faded ball in the back gutter and put a rectangular patch of ball marks on the fence opposite. Climbing onto the roof reveals where the last serve went.
8. **An enamel wash trough** — Put a chipped trough, crooked pipe clip and single replacement tile on the back wall below the laundry sash. The chips and fittings draw attention to the newly accessible side of the building.
9. **A translucent shed roof sheet** — Replace one solid corrugated strip with milky fibreglass. Let it cast a broad rectangle of daylight across the workbench so the repair in progress catches the eye from the door.
10. **A two-stage paint job** — Give the brick house extension one fresh coat up to ladder height, with a crisp masking edge and the old colour above. A player on the roof can spot where the painter called it a day.

## Gardens with recognisable plants and wildlife

A St Albans family photo shows a grape pergola whose owner grew and wove cane for his harvest baskets. Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria records deeply furrowed dark bark on red ironbark. BirdLife describes the Willie Wagtail hunting on open lawns and flicking its tail sideways. These give the artist a specific canopy, trunk and movement to draw. [St Albans grape harvest](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/1950200) · [VicFlora ironbark](https://vicflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/flora/taxon/46b30d64-1f21-4d71-a770-027910050d6c) · [BirdLife Willie Wagtail](https://birdlife.org.au/bird-profiles/willie-wagtail/)

11. **A grape pergola over the neighbours’ paving** — Run a twisted trunk up one post, hang a few bunches under the canopy and cast broad leaf-shaped shadows on pale concrete. It should read as a single shady destination from the roof.
12. **An ironbark beside a ribbon gum** — Give the pool-end tree a dark, grooved trunk and its neighbour a pale fork with two long curls of shed bark. The pair gives players a reliable “dark tree” and “white tree” callout.
13. **Gaps in the gum crowns** — Arrange narrow sprays of leaves on a few uneven branch forks. Preserve pockets of sky between them, especially around the fork above the possum box, so the branches read from the lawn.
14. **A lemon tree leaning towards the kitchen** — Grow one dense, low crown against the kitchen wall with a few matte yellow fruit tucked inside. The low spreading shape breaks up the row of tall gums.
15. **A red bottlebrush verge** — Plant a low cluster of red brush flowers near the outer pool fence, with tawny seed heads along its front edge. A splash of red marks the pool approach while the open lane stays clear.
16. **Cicada shells at the ladder** — Attach two amber, split-backed shells to the timber beside the tank ladder, one at eye level. They reward the pause before the climb and suggest a hot summer afternoon.
17. **A Willie Wagtail on the cut grass** — Give a black-and-white wagtail two short dashes after insects and a sideways tail flick when it stops. Its low silhouette and movement distinguish it from the birdbath magpies.
18. **A flower that dips under a bird** — Let a small nectar-feeding bird land on the far grevillea, bend one flower stem and leave it slowly springing upright. The branch tells the story even after the bird has gone.
19. **An exposed root line** — At the base of a mature gum, run two raised roots through dry soil and collect leaves in the hollows. Carry the root shapes into the ground colour so the tree feels planted.
20. **An interrupted harvest** — Hang a half-full cane grape basket from the pergola, with a loose repaired weave and a single fallen bunch below it. The close view tells you someone put it down in a hurry.

## The household behind the party

On *Grounded*, assistant art director Paul Fish describes the team inventing product names and designing logos for them. A fictional family brand can make Marcus’s BBQ props recognisable across several scenes while giving the artist control over the colours and lettering. [Grounded artist interview](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/grounded-release-concept-art-obsidian/amp)

21. **A produce hatch between neighbours** — Fit a little sliding hatch to the dividing fence beyond the main yard, with two paper bags of lemons bearing different initials. The scene gives the two households a relationship.
22. **Dazza’s footy-tomato sauce** — Draw a tomato shaped like an Aussie rules football on a squeeze bottle at the grill. Repeat it as a large, sun-bleached crate print by the shed so players can recognise the same joke at two distances.
23. **A shelf of backyard trophies** — Mount a painted stump, an upside-down wooden spoon and a dented cup in increasing order of grandeur on the verandah. Three big shapes do the work even when the plaques are too small to read.
24. **A fictional footy club fixture** — Tape a sun-faded fixture sheet beside the back door with one match circled and a strip of paper torn away. Give the club a stripe you can pick out from the bar.
25. **A party invitation under a fridge magnet** — Dress the outdoor drinks fridge with a hand-drawn blob on an invitation and a magnet holding only one corner. The lifting corner catches the side light when a player reaches for a drink.
26. **The preserving shelf** — Set plum and amber preserving jars just inside a kitchen pane. Backlight them with a single warm patch so they have translucent-looking edges in both builds.
27. **A pool-height pencil chart** — Put a few dated height marks on the pool-gate post, ending in a suspiciously high mark labelled “Dazza”. It is a small joke to find on the walk back from a swim.
28. **A pie cooling behind the flyscreen** — Give the back-door sill an enamel plate and a pie with a single missing wedge. An angled strip of daylight across the crust makes the silhouette readable through the mesh.
29. **The neighbours notice a miss** — After a throw lands near one designated window, raise the blind a handspan, reveal two curious eyes and lower it again. The silent reaction happens after impact.
30. **A grandstand scarf for the gnomes** — During the results screen, drape a miniature scarf in the winning team’s colours across one existing spectator’s lap. Revisiting the gnomes gives the result a little epilogue.

## Light, surfaces and movement at player height

The wheelbarrow is a concrete repair candidate. In the Three.js source its tub has a translated parent, while several handles and weeds use the world-space rod helper with local coordinates. The mismatch follows from the code. Its appearance on a live browser build still needs a visual check. [Browser yard source](../public/index.html)

31. **Reassemble the browser wheelbarrow** — Attach its handles, legs, trowel grip and loose weeds to the same positioned group as the tub. Check the loaded barrow beside the garden and the clear grass near the world origin.
32. **One gust across the yard** — Send a light gust from the west fence across grass tips, a low gum branch and then the hanging washing, with a short delay at each. Players can follow the breeze all the way across the scene.
33. **Mower turns at the end of a stripe** — Curve the cut marks where the driver turned, press two tyre tracks into the lawn texture and leave a little ridge of clippings. The existing mower now explains the pattern in the grass.
34. **Wet steps out of the pool** — Stamp three or four shrinking footprints on the pavers when a wet blob climbs out. Use a bare footprint or thong sole to match the character and fade them over the next few seconds.
35. **Verandah shade you can play in** — Cool and soften the light under the overhang while leaving a pale patch of wall behind passing blobs. Test a dark singlet and a flying teddy against that wall from the bar.
36. **Stop distant roof and fence sparkle** — Broaden or fade fine ribs, screen mesh and bright fence fixings as they recede. Review a sideways camera run by the Heist pool and at the roof edge to find which surfaces still shimmer.
37. **Worn metal on the roof route** — Add a broad scuffed patch on the tank lip and a satin-bright replaced sheet at the actual roof landing. Those two marks show first-timers where people have made the jump.
38. **A waterline that meets the coping** — Darken the lower inside face of the pool tiles and let a thin band of reflected light move across it. The meeting point of water and concrete stays visible while the surface ripples.
39. **A trampoline mat that takes the weight** — Depress the mat under the landing blob and let the closest spring band tighten and release on the existing bounce. The launch reads from across the yard.
40. **A slow cloud shadow over the far houses** — Move one broad, soft shadow across the back row and let a roof flash warm as it emerges. The skyline gains motion while lighting stays steady over the central throwing lane.

## Blobs, throws and Teddy Heist

Valve used architecture and material as well as colour to distinguish bases in *Team Fortress 2*. Mediatonic artist Ash Kerins recommends viewing a *Fall Guys* design zoomed out and partly hidden to check whether it remains recognisable. Those are practical tests for this game’s changing bases, low walls and small blobs. [Valve’s rendering paper, sections 4.2–4.3](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf) · [Fall Guys character art notes](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=224711)

41. **Heist junctions you can call by name** — Face one fixed junction in diagonal timber lattice, one in tall tin ribs and one in wide horizontal boards. Give each treatment two visible faces so a runner recognises “lattice” on the way in and the way home after the bases rotate.
42. **A teddy-shaped dent on the bank shelf** — As the teddy leaves, reveal a pressed outline in the cushion underneath. Arriving defenders can see at a glance why the shelf is empty.
43. **The teddy’s floppy ear on sharp turns** — Add a pale stitched muzzle and one ear that swings out and settles against the carrier on a turn. A defender gets a clear side-on glimpse of what the runner is holding.
44. **A mechanical bank counter** — Flip a large painted number once above the shelf when a bank is awarded. The scorer and nearby opponents can both see the moment the point lands.
45. **Singlets that read over a low wall** — Try a sash, two broad hoops and a shoulder block while keeping a solid team-colour panel and the back name. Compare the designs with half the torso hidden behind Heist cover.
46. **A heavyweight catch pose** — After an existing catch succeeds, pull the elbows in for a heavy item or let the noodle fold over one shoulder. The same timing produces different comic performances.
47. **A bot brushes itself off** — During the existing recovery, let a bot pluck one blade of grass from its singlet and glance towards the thrower. Players can recognise a recovery from a purposeful gesture.
48. **Thrown items against bright sky and dark timber** — Give the teddy a light outer seam and the can a broad dark stripe. Check both at cross-yard distance against sky, lawn and fence so the player can track the item through an entire arc.
49. **Boot pivots at a maze corner** — As a bot brakes into an existing turn, briefly plant its outside foot and let shoulders lead the exit. A pursuer can read the change of direction from the body before the bot accelerates away.
50. **A results photo at the BBQ** — Frame the round winner and runner-up beside Dazza with a slightly crooked instant-photo border and the existing score handwritten below. Use the actual round result and the selected blob appearances so every picture belongs to that match.

## Eight improvements from Marcus’s playtest

The current sprinkler draws five glints in each implementation. In Polished Rust the bench by the bins has legs ending at 0.78 m, while the upper back board sits at 1.25 m. The separate shed workbench already has collision. These findings explain which objects to change. [Native sprinkler and bench construction](../rust/crates/bbq_core/src/looks_yard.rs) · [Browser sprinkler](../public/index.html) · [Yard colliders](../rust/crates/bbq_core/src/yard.rs)

For a budget-friendly spray, Epic documents continuous particle ribbons and velocity-aligned sprites. NVIDIA’s grass chapter covers rendering groups of blades and varying wind; its water chapter describes layering small surface ripples over larger motion. Clemson’s high-speed laboratory observations show the distinct stages of a crown, collapsing sheet and separated droplets. These inform the timing and shapes below. Khronos also documents how coincident surfaces can alternate in the depth buffer, one cause to check for the reported flicker. [Epic particle rendering](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/render-module-reference-for-niagara-effects-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) · [NVIDIA grass](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-7-rendering-countless-blades-waving-grass) · [NVIDIA water](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models) · [Clemson splash observations](https://cecas.clemson.edu/~jsaylor/saylor.rsch.dropImpacts.html) · [Khronos depth precision](https://wikis.khronos.org/opengl/Depth_Buffer_Precision)

51. **Make the sprinkler throw a natural fan of water** — Start narrow at the nozzle, split into curved streams, then stretch droplets along their direction of travel as gravity pulls them down. Let water already in the air finish its arc after the head swings past. From across the lawn you should see the sweep, with fine mist and dark wet grass visible up close.
52. **Make the grass look like a real mown lawn** — Blend the flat ground colour with small irregular clumps of tapered blades, darker roots and occasional straw tips. Keep the middle short and trampled, leave longer edges around posts, and fade distant blades into the ground texture. Judge it from eye height in motion and from the roof, where obvious tile repeats show up.
53. **Fix the remaining flicker in motion** — Record the same slow sideways walk by the Heist pool, walls and yard edges. Isolate overlapping surfaces, crawling shadow edges and sparkling fine textures one at a time. Remove or merge coplanar layers, adjust shadows where they crawl, and filter distant detail where it aliases. Compare before-and-after clips on desktop and browser.
54. **Support the back boards on the bench by the bins** — Continue the rear legs into two slightly reclined uprights tall enough to meet both boards. Put dark bolt heads at the actual wood joints and keep the existing missing slat. The rear and side views should show how the back carries weight.
55. **Give water balloons a more dramatic, directional splash** — Orient a broad, fast-opening sheet against the surface struck, follow with a handful of heavier drops that arc down, then briefly darken the contact patch. Flatten the fan along a wall, wrap it around a hit blob and show a lower splash on lawn. Aim for a readable split-second impact with a clear view for the next throw.
56. **Send ripples out from a player entering the pool** — On the instant the body crosses the surface, centre a few expanding rings on the entry point. A slow walk-in makes a small disturbance and a hard landing makes wider, higher-contrast rings. Clip them at the coping and let them fade into the water while the swimmer is still in it.
57. **Make the bench by the bins solid** — Match player collision to the visible seat and upright back. Walk at it from front, rear, and both ends, then jump onto the seat and step off. Verify the gap to the bins stays passable for the normal player width, and create the matching visible bench wherever its collision is enabled.

58. **Give the entire outer neighbourhood distinct households** — Give each visible row its own building kit. Try a cream brick duplex with separate rear doors and awnings, a weatherboard cottage with a glazed sleepout, and a patched workshop with high clerestory windows. Group two signs of occupation per lot, such as a child's bike beside a tiny porch or a hard-used loading door beside stacked pallets. From the arena each place should read by outline and colour; from the ridge the room layouts, extensions and objects should explain who uses it.
