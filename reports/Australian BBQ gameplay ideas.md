# Turn this backyard into a party

Australian BBQ can stand out by making an ordinary Aussie backyard actively participate in the slapstick: props, characters and landmarks should react to throws, carries and objectives in ways players can read and laugh at. Similar games show that a memorable arena does not need every object to be destructible; Gang Beasts makes a few stage features central, while Party Animals and Totally Reliable Delivery Service use mode-specific objectives and playful side interactions. For this game, I recommend first enriching existing actions with brief visual reactions, then improving Teddy Heist carrier and route readability, and only then testing one new backyard interaction such as a telegraphed sprinkler strip. These ideas preserve the current throw-and-catch identity, 1v1 with no bots, equal character movement and hit sizes, and Teddy Heist’s rule that only banked teddies score. The research supports what those games contain and how their developers describe them; it does not establish which feature will make this game more popular. Treat the rankings below as practical design hypotheses to validate with local play.

## Party brawlers make the arena part of the joke

Gang Beasts foregrounds grabs and shoves around a handful of dangerous, distinct stage features; Rubber Bandits makes stealing loot and escaping through destructible arenas part of its heist fantasy. Party Animals shows a different path: objective modes can give teams a clear reason to move objects, while map updates add low-stakes activities and set dressing. Those examples point to a useful distinction for Australian BBQ: borrow the *principle* of a recognizable map feature creating a choice, not the rivals’ industrial hazards, weapon catalogues or destruction systems. ([Gang Beasts](https://gangbeasts.game/), [Rubber Bandits](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1206610/RubberBandits/), [Party Animals developer log](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?enddate=1656470308&feed=steam_community_announcements), [Party Animals updates](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260320/Party_Animals/))

Human: Fall Flat emphasizes multiple routes through physics levels, Ultimate Chicken Horse makes hazard placement interesting because the builders must face the same course, and Totally Reliable Delivery Service presents vehicles and toys as distractions within an interactive sandbox. The shared lesson is readable counterplay: show where a route goes, telegraph what a toy will do, and let both sides respond. Random knockouts or invisible effects would work against Australian BBQ’s readable throwing and stun rules. ([Human: Fall Flat](https://store.steampowered.com/app/477160/Human_Fall_Flat/), [Ultimate Chicken Horse](https://noodlecake.com/games/ultimate-chicken-horse/), [Totally Reliable Delivery Service](https://www.totallyreliable.com/))

## The backyard is Australian BBQ’s strongest original asset

The project already has an unusually specific setting to build on: Dazza and the BBQ, the esky, picnic shade, pool, trampoline, clothesline, magpies, ute, Smoko shelter and a Teddy Heist maze. Its design notes also protect open fighting routes, no-bot 1v1 play, equal character speed and hit sizes, non-stacking stuns, and bank-only Heist scoring. That suggests the best ideas make those objects and routines matter more, rather than adding generic weapons or another knockout mode. ([project instructions](../AGENTS.md), [game checklist](../CHECKLIST.md), [graphics backlog](../CONCEPT_ART_GRAPHICS_BACKLOG.md))

The following order balances originality with the size of the implementation risk:

| Rank | Idea | Why it could be fun; visual hook | Effort and mode fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · First slice** | **Backyard reaction layer** | Make existing moments land: a caught item gets a compact catch flash/pose, a hard landing kicks up a short dust puff, magpies hop or scatter after a nearby impact, and Dazza turns or gestures when the BBQ area is disrupted. Keep effects behind blobs and items. | **Low–medium.** Mostly presentation and animation. Fits FFA, Teams and Smoko; use reactions only in Heist if they do not obscure teddies or routes. No new scoring or voice lines. |
| **2** | **Teddy Heist route and carrier cues** | Give maze lanes distinct landmarks and bases clear icon-and-shape signage. A carrier should read instantly through a visible carry pose or small dust/tuft trail. This makes chases and callouts easier without changing the maze or score rule. | **Low for visual landmarks; medium for carry effects.** Especially useful in Heist and online Teams. Keep team identity readable in shade and do not rely on colour alone. |
| **3** | **One telegraphed sprinkler strip** | A handle/indicator warns that a marked lawn edge is about to spray; a brief, obvious water arc and wet patch make the yard feel alive and create a route choice. Start with visual feedback only, then test a short, consistent low-friction slide if the cosmetic version adds enough anticipation. | **Medium.** Test in FFA/Teams away from spawns and bases. Keep it off in Heist until proven fair; do not use hidden or random timing. If it affects movement, it needs host/guest synchronization checks. |
| **4** | **Esky or picnic interaction** | Open an existing esky to reveal one visible, known refill, or let a chair shift a little when bumped. A lid animation and contents marker explain the affordance; a short reset state prevents farming. | **Low–medium, but inspect the existing R interaction first.** Best in FFA/Teams. Avoid score rewards or random loot, and do not let it alter Heist banking. |
| **5 · Later** | **A short backyard party series** | Host-selected, fast rounds can rotate between current modes and map variants, with friendly end-of-round cards such as “longest carry,” “most catches,” or “closest escape.” This makes the cast and play history feel social without an MVP-only winner screen. | **Medium–high.** Fits private groups and preserves host-led play, but adds menus, round transitions and more content. Do after the current maps’ interactions have been playtested. |

These are design inferences, not confirmed features in the reference games. The repo’s current code should be inspected before choosing the esky interaction or any online effect; the research did not verify their implementation cost in Bevy.

## Small reactions can create more memorable play than more props

The first prototype should add a few short reactions to existing events, not fill the arena with extra clutter. For example, the same hit could briefly stir dust from dry ground, make bunting or a sign sway, and draw a magpie’s attention; a Teddy Heist bank could give the shelter sign a short, clear celebration. These reactions make the backyard feel responsive and help players understand what just happened, while keeping physics, damage, scoring and networking rules intact. Similar games demonstrate the value of distinct stage features and small interactive activities, but the specific reactions here are original proposals that need playtesting. ([Gang Beasts stage examples](https://steamcommunity.com/app/285900/announcements/), [Party Animals map updates](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?enddate=1789479130&feed=steam_community_announcements))

For Teddy Heist, prioritize route landmarks and carrier visibility over new hazards. Use both shape and colour to label the bases and lanes, and ensure the carried teddy stays visible against the character and scenery. This builds on the requested laser-tag-style maze while leaving routes and banking rules intact. For FFA and Teams, the same environmental storytelling can be looser: the BBQ and shelter can react to a nearby scuffle, while the pool and trampoline produce distinct, brief effects when used.

## New arena mechanics should telegraph and reset

A sprinkler is a better first new gameplay interaction than destructible walls or a swinging machine because it can live on the yard’s edge, have one visible activation cue and affect a small, fixed patch. Start with the spray and wet mark alone. If local matches show that players notice and enjoy it, test a short shared slide effect with a clear duration, a predictable reset and the same behavior for every player. That keeps the idea funny and tactical without deciding a fight through an unseen random hazard.

Keep full arena destruction, moving platforms and player-placed traps for much later. They can create spectacle in other games, but they would increase collision, exploit and online synchronization work while competing with the current throw-and-catch loop. Ultimate Chicken Horse’s design at least keeps builders exposed to their own hazards; any future BBQ hazard should follow that same counterplay principle. ([Ultimate Chicken Horse](https://noodlecake.com/games/ultimate-chicken-horse/), [Gang Beasts announcements](https://steamcommunity.com/app/285900/announcements/))

## Conclusion

The clearest path to originality is to make Australian BBQ’s backyard behave like a comic character: the objects, Dazza and the magpies notice what players do, while each mode gives those same places a different purpose. Start with a presentation-only reaction pass and Heist readability, then let a local playtest decide whether the sprinkler earns a gameplay effect. This sequence gives the game a more distinctive personality before committing to the heavier cost of new physics, hazards or modes.
